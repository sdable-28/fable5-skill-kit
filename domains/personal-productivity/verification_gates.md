# Verification Gates — Personal Productivity & Executive Function

Pass/fail criteria specific to this domain. Apply the relevant gates before declaring any productivity system, intervention, decision-support artifact, or audit "done." A deliverable that fails any applicable gate is not done, regardless of how complete it looks.

---

## Gate 1 — Capture Integrity (Leak Test)

**What passes:** The person, asked "name one commitment you've made that is not in the system," searches and comes up empty. Spot-check confirms: 5 commitments sampled from the last 2 weeks of email/chat/meeting notes all appear in the trusted system with current status.

**What fails:** Any commitment found living only in the head, in an unprocessed inbox older than 48 hours, or in a second "shadow system" (starred emails, sticky notes, saved Slack messages used as a task list).

**How to check:** Ask the question directly. Then independently sample recent communication channels for asks/promises and cross-reference against the system. Both checks must pass; self-report alone is insufficient.

---

## Gate 2 — Next-Action Concreteness

**What passes:** 5 randomly sampled items from the active next-action list each (a) start with a physical verb, (b) name a single executable behavior, (c) could be started within 60 seconds of reading with no further what-do-I-do thinking.

**What fails:** Any sampled item that is a project in disguise ("Taxes"), a person ("Mom"), an outcome ("Get fit"), or a multi-step blob ("Research and book venue and send invites"). One failure in five = gate fails; the clarify step is leaking.

**How to check:** Random sample, not user-selected. Read each item aloud and ask "what exactly would your hands do first?" — hesitation is a fail.

---

## Gate 3 — Output Over Tidiness

**What passes:** The week's review answers "what important work was completed?" with named deliverables traceable to stated priorities, BEFORE any discussion of list hygiene. MIT selection-to-completion rate ≥60% over the trailing 4 weeks.

**What fails:** A week reported as productive on the evidence of clean lists, reorganized tools, or inbox state, with an empty or unrelated output ledger. MIT completion <40% while small-task completions are high (small-task binging). Any week where system-maintenance time exceeded deep-work time.

**How to check:** Compare the output ledger against the MITs selected at each week's start. Compute the ratio. Check time spent on tool/system fiddling against time in held deep blocks.

---

## Gate 4 — Energy-Schedule Alignment

**What passes:** An energy map exists (self-reported peaks/troughs from ≥5 recent workdays), and deep-work blocks sit inside the peak window on the majority of workdays. Administrative batches sit in troughs.

**What fails:** A schedule designed without an energy map. Deep work systematically placed in troughs "because that's what's free." Any recommendation to power through a trough with willpower instead of redesigning placement.

**How to check:** Ask for the energy map's source data and date (>90 days old = stale, re-collect). Overlay it on the actual calendar — not the aspirational template — for the last week.

---

## Gate 5 — Deep-Work Block Authenticity

**What passes:** Blocks are ≥90 minutes, have one named deliverable, and were executed with notifications off and communication channels closed. Quota adherence ≥60% over the trial window (blocks may move; the weekly quota may not silently shrink). Intrusions handled by ≤10-second capture.

**What fails:** "Deep work" blocks with chat open, blocks under 45 minutes labeled deep, blocks with no named deliverable, or a calendar showing blocks that are routinely scheduled and never held (scheduling theater).

**How to check:** For last week's blocks: held or cancelled? During held blocks, was anything sent on chat/email (verifiable by timestamps)? What deliverable came out of each?

---

## Gate 6 — Decision Journal Validity

**What passes:** Every consequential decision in the review period has an entry dated BEFORE the outcome was knowable, containing: context, alternatives considered, expected outcome with an explicit probability, reversibility classification, and a scheduled review date. Past-due reviews are scored (expected vs. actual).

**What fails:** Entries written after outcomes are known and not tagged RETRO. Entries without probabilities (uncalibratable). Journals containing only decisions that went well. Review dates that exist but are never executed. One-way-door decisions with no pre-mortem attached.

**How to check:** Compare entry timestamps to outcome dates. Count entries with numeric probabilities. Check the ratio of scored-to-due reviews — below 50% means the journal is a diary, not an instrument.

---

## Gate 7 — Reversibility-Proportional Analysis

**What passes:** For the last 5 significant decisions: reversible decisions were resolved in a single sitting or less; irreversible decisions show a pre-mortem, at least one base rate or outside view, and at least one night between analysis and commitment.

**What fails:** Days of deliberation on a two-way door (tool choice, trial subscription, meeting format). A one-way door (hire, quit, public commitment, contract) decided in one pass with inside-view reasoning only. Analysis budget uncorrelated or inversely correlated with undo cost.

**How to check:** For each decision, ask "what would undoing this cost?" then compare to the time and rigor actually spent. Check the journal for the pre-mortem artifact on one-way doors.

---

## Gate 8 — Meeting Audit Currency

**What passes:** Every recurring meeting on the calendar has, within the last quarter: a named owner, a one-sentence written purpose, a computed annual cost (attendees × duration × frequency × loaded hourly value), and a keep/shrink/async/kill verdict. New recurring invites carry a decay date.

**What fails:** Any recurring meeting whose purpose the attendee cannot state in one sentence. Audit older than one quarter. Recurring meetings that survived the audit by default rather than by explicit keep verdict. Attendance maintained by silent no-shows instead of formal decline.

**How to check:** Pick three recurring meetings at random; demand purpose and cost from memory or from the audit doc. Check the audit doc's date.

---

## Gate 9 — Intervention Falsifiability

**What passes:** Every proposed habit, tool, or schedule change ships with: the single behavior changing, its trigger, an observable success signal, a kill criterion, a review date ≤14 days out, and a defined minimum-viable degraded mode for high-load days. Only one intervention active per two-week window per subsystem.

**What fails:** "Try this and see how it feels." Multiple simultaneous changes (attribution impossible). No kill criterion (hope, not experiment). No degraded mode (designed for the idealized self). Trial windows that contained no high-load day and are declared validated anyway.

**How to check:** Read the intervention spec. All six fields present? Was the review actually executed on the review date? Did the trial window include at least one realistically bad day?

---

## Gate 10 — Weekly Review Liveness

**What passes:** Weekly Review completed within the last 10 days, in ≤60 minutes, following a written agenda that includes: output ledger, calendar look-back and look-ahead, list spot-check (Gate 2 sample), leak test question, and next week's MIT selection. A 15-minute triage version exists and was used on any week the full version couldn't run.

**What fails:** Last review >14 days ago (system is declared dead — recovery protocol required, not a quiet resume). Reviews that are pure list-grooming with no output ledger. Reviews consistently running >90 minutes (will decay; shrink it). Two consecutive misses treated as a lapse instead of a design failure.

**How to check:** Date of last completed review and its duration. Does the agenda artifact exist? Was anything from the review actually changed (MITs set, stale items killed) — a review that changes nothing is a ritual.

---

## Gate 11 — AI Offload Boundary

**What passes:** Recurring AI uses are enumerated, and each falls on the correct side of the boundary: retrieval, formatting, first drafts, enumeration, and stress-testing offloaded; final judgment, priority calls, emotionally significant communication, and actively-trained skills retained. For judgment tasks, the human's own conclusion exists before AI critique. Recurring prompts have templates with format and length ceilings, and net minutes saved is positive.

**What fails:** Decisions executed on AI recommendation the human cannot re-derive in their own words. Prompts degrading toward "handle this." AI output rubber-stamped without evaluation. Emotionally significant messages sent verbatim from AI drafts. Recurring AI interactions that cost more attention than they return.

**How to check:** List the last 10 AI-assisted tasks; classify each against the boundary. For one judgment task, ask the human to state the reasoning unaided. Inspect two recurring prompts for format/length constraints.

---

## Gate 12 — Communication Efficiency

**What passes:** Email/chat processed in 2–3 fixed daily windows using do/delegate/defer/delete; outside windows, notifications off. Outbound asks follow context → ask → by-when and fit on one screen. Meeting requests from the person carry a written brief; meeting requests to the person without one are returned for a brief or converted to async.

**What fails:** Continuous inbox monitoring (response latency <5 min all day = always watching). Outbound messages where the ask is buried below paragraph three or has no deadline. Meetings scheduled for anything a document plus comments could settle. First work action of the day being the inbox on 3+ of 5 days.

**How to check:** Message timestamps across a sample day (are sends clustered in windows?). Read the person's last 5 outbound asks against the brief structure. Count last week's meetings that produced a decision or artifact a doc couldn't have.
