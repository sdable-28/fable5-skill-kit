# Common Patterns — Founder & Entrepreneur

## 1. Idea Validation Sprint
**Trigger:** New idea, pivot candidate, or new feature line pitched as "everyone wants this" with zero paying evidence.
**Approach:** 1. Define the JTBD in one sentence: who hires this product, for what job, firing what alternative. 2. Run 10+ mom-test interviews — past behavior only ("when did you last hit this? what did you do? what did it cost?"), never "would you use/pay." 3. Map competitors in three rings: direct, indirect, status quo (spreadsheet/intern/do-nothing). Status quo is the real enemy. 4. Size bottoms-up: reachable buyers × plausible price × realistic penetration. Ignore top-down TAM slides. 5. Demand a costly commitment before building: prepayment, signed LOI, or scheduled pilot.
**Checklist:** [ ] JTBD one-liner written [ ] 10 interviews, past-behavior only [ ] Current spend on the problem quantified [ ] 3-ring competitor map incl. status quo [ ] Bottoms-up TAM math shown [ ] ≥3 costly commitments secured.
**Variation:** Polite interest counted as validation. Compliments and "I'd totally use that" are zeros. Only money, time, or reputation spent counts. Also watch for "we can build it" masquerading as "the market needs it."

## 2. Co-founder Equity & Agreement Setup
**Trigger:** Two or more people start working together seriously — before incorporation, first raise, or first revenue. Do this before the first conflict, not after.
**Approach:** 1. Reject default 50/50; score contributions explicitly: idea origination (low weight), full-time vs part-time, capital in, domain expertise, opportunity cost, who is CEO. 2. Put ALL founder equity on 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff — including your own. 3. Write the co-founder agreement now: roles, decision rights, tiebreaker, what happens on departure (good/bad leaver), IP assignment, non-compete scope. 4. Assign every pre-existing relevant IP to the entity in writing. 5. Agree the parting protocol: buyback terms, board seat removal, announcement wording.
**Checklist:** [ ] Weighted split, not reflex 50/50 [ ] 4yr/1yr-cliff vesting for ALL founders [ ] Written roles + final-decision owner per area [ ] Departure/buyback terms signed [ ] IP assignment executed [ ] Deadlock tiebreaker named.
**Variation:** Agreement drafted but never signed because "we trust each other" — it then gets negotiated during the first fight, at maximum leverage asymmetry. Sign before money or traction arrives.

## 3. Incorporation & Cap Table Hygiene
**Trigger:** About to sign a customer contract, hire anyone, accept any outside money, or split equity.
**Approach:** 1. If venture-scale: Delaware C-corp, now — not LLC-then-convert during a raise. If lifestyle/cashflow business: LLC in home state is fine. 2. Founders purchase shares at par and file 83(b) within 30 days — this deadline is unrecoverable. 3. Model the cap table from day 1 in a single source of truth (Carta or one spreadsheet): founders, option pool (10–15%, sized to the 18-month hiring plan, created pre-money so founders eat the dilution knowingly), first-hire grants (engineer #1 ~0.5–1.5%). 4. Classify workers correctly: contractors get IP-assignment contracts; anyone working core hours under your direction is an employee. 5. Every equity promise goes in writing on the cap table same week — no verbal grants.
**Checklist:** [ ] Entity matches funding path [ ] 83(b) filed ≤30 days, proof saved [ ] Option pool sized to hiring plan [ ] All IP assigned to entity [ ] Contractor/employee classification reviewed [ ] Zero verbal equity promises outstanding.
**Variation:** The forgotten early promise — "you'll get 2%" told to an advisor or friend, never papered, resurfacing as a claim during Series A diligence and stalling the round.

## 4. Term Sheet / SAFE Review
**Trigger:** Any investment document arrives — SAFE, convertible note, or priced-round term sheet.
**Approach:** 1. Separate the terms into three buckets before reacting to valuation: ECONOMICS (cap, discount, price, liquidation preference, option pool location, anti-dilution), CONTROL (board seats, protective provisions, drag-along, voting), NOISE (legalese defaults). 2. Model dilution: convert every SAFE at its cap plus the new pool into a pro-forma cap table before signing anything new. 3. Flag killers: >1x or participating liquidation preference, full-ratchet anti-dilution, investor board control at seed, broad protective provisions covering ordinary operations, MFN clauses you can't track. 4. Check pool shuffle: a higher valuation with a bigger pre-money pool can net you less than a lower valuation. 5. Reference-check the investor with 2 founders they backed through a DOWN period.
**Checklist:** [ ] Pro-forma cap table with all SAFEs converted [ ] Liquidation pref = 1x non-participating [ ] Anti-dilution = broad-based weighted average [ ] Board composition post-close mapped [ ] Protective provisions list read line by line [ ] Pool pre/post-money position priced in [ ] 2 down-cycle founder references done.
**Variation:** Negotiating headline valuation while conceding control terms. A 2x participating preference or investor-controlled board costs more than 20% on the cap — economics are recoverable, control terms rarely are.

## 5. First-10-Customers Manual Grind
**Trigger:** Product exists (or barely does) and revenue is $0–$10k MRR; or the team is drafting a "growth strategy" before any stranger has paid.
**Approach:** 1. Founder sells personally — no salespeople, no ads, no PLG funnel yet. 2. Build a named list of 50 targets who visibly have the problem; contact each with a specific claim about their pain, not a product pitch. 3. Deliver manually and unscalably: onboard by hand, sit in their workflow, do parts of the job yourself. 4. After each sale/loss, write down the exact objection or the exact phrase that closed — that is the future landing page and sales script. 5. Only after ~$10k MRR of manual, repeatable sales do you automate the motion that already works.
**Checklist:** [ ] 50-name target list [ ] Founder on every call [ ] Manual onboarding for first 10 [ ] Win/loss note per prospect [ ] Repeated closing phrase identified [ ] $10k MRR before any paid acquisition.
**Variation:** Friendly first customers who pay out of goodwill and never complain — their silence masks a bad product. Weight the churned and the cold-outbound wins; discount friends, ex-colleagues, and investor intros as evidence of PMF.

## 6. Post-Raise Burn & Hiring Plan
**Trigger:** Money just landed, board or team pressure to "deploy capital" and hire fast; or runway math has never been written down.
**Approach:** 1. Fix the runway target first: raise-to-milestone, minimum 24 months or to the next fundable proof point, whichever is stricter. 2. Sequence hires against validated bottlenecks only — a hire is justified by a broken, repeating process, not by an org-chart template or investor pattern-matching. 3. Freeze all pre-PMF "scale" hires: VP Sales, growth marketers, second product line. 4. Set a monthly survival dashboard: net burn, months of runway, MRR, retention — and review it before any press, awards, or conference decisions. 5. Pre-commit the cut plan: at what runway number do you freeze hiring, and at what number do you cut.
**Checklist:** [ ] ≥24mo runway or milestone-funded plan [ ] Each hire tied to a named recurring bottleneck [ ] No sales/growth leadership pre-PMF [ ] Monthly burn dashboard live [ ] Runway trigger points for freeze/cut written down.
**Variation:** Confusing investor enthusiasm for market demand — raising big, then spending to the raise instead of to revenue. Headcount tripled against a demand curve that never materialized is the standard 18-months-later death.
