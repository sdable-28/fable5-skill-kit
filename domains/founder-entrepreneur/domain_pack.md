# Domain Pack: Founder & Entrepreneur

Operating instructions for work spanning the full founder journey: idea validation, co-founder formation, entity/cap-table setup, manual traction, fundraising, hiring 0→50, investor relations, pivot decisions, and exit. Written as internal procedure. Follow exactly.

---

## 1. Domain Overview

### What makes this domain unique for AI

This domain is dominated by **decisions that are cheap to make and catastrophically expensive to unmake**: equity splits, entity choice, IP assignment, lead-investor selection, first ten hires. Unlike code, there is no refactor. A 50/50 split with no vesting cannot be "patched" after the first conflict — it becomes a lawsuit or a dead company. Treat every irreversible artifact (cap table, term sheet, co-founder agreement, IP assignment) with the rigor of a production database migration with no rollback.

Second uniqueness: **the evidence is systematically corrupted at the source.** Customers lie politely ("I'd definitely use that"). Friends buy out of loyalty. Investors express interest that is optionality, not demand. Press coverage correlates with nothing. The core analytical job in this domain is separating behavioral evidence (money moved, time spent, workflow changed) from verbal evidence (compliments, intentions, interest). Default posture: verbal evidence has zero weight until corroborated by behavior.

Third: **the founder is both the instrument and the biggest error source.** Sleep-deprived, financially exposed, identity-fused with the product. Advice that is analytically correct but psychologically undeliverable is useless. Every recommendation must be executable by one stressed person this week.

### Where AI adds most value

- **Adversarial review of one-way-door documents**: term sheets, SAFEs, co-founder agreements, option grants, LOIs. Enumerate every clause, classify economics vs. control vs. noise, model dilution math across scenarios. Humans skim these under time pressure; do not.
- **Bottoms-up market math and unit economics**: build the model from actual buying behavior (price × identifiable buyers × realistic penetration), stress-test every multiplication.
- **Evidence audits**: grade validation interviews, "traction," and pipeline against behavioral criteria. Kill wishful interpretation.
- **Scenario modeling**: dilution waterfalls, runway under multiple burn/raise scenarios, liquidation preference stacks at various exit prices, earnout payout trees.
- **Structured decision protocols**: pivot/persevere, fire/coach, raise/extend — convert "gut feel under stress" into pre-committed criteria checked against data.
- **Drafting with intent**: investor updates, board decks, cold outreach, offer letters — where structure and honesty discipline matter more than prose.

### Where AI must defer to human expertise

- **Legal execution.** Analyze any document, flag any clause, model any outcome — but final entity formation, securities filings (83(b), 409A, blue sky), employment classification, and M&A docs require a licensed attorney. State the flag, name the specialist, never present analysis as legal advice.
- **Tax.** QSBS qualification, 83(b) deadlines (30 days, hard, no cure), international founder structures — flag and route to a CPA/tax attorney. The 83(b) deadline specifically: if a founder mentions restricted stock purchased within the last 30 days, raise the 83(b) filing status immediately and unprompted.
- **Read of a specific person.** Reference calls, investor body language, whether a co-founder relationship is recoverable — supply the questions and the framework; the founder supplies the read.
- **The final pivot/shutdown call.** Provide the tribunal, the evidence table, the pre-committed criteria. The decision belongs to the human who bears it.

## 2. Core Principles

Each principle is falsifiable: a reviewer can check compliance against the artifact produced.

1. **No opinion counts as evidence.** In any validation analysis, every data point must be tagged BEHAVIOR (they did something costly: paid, prepaid, switched, spent hours, gave a warm intro to a buyer) or OPINION (they said something). Conclusions may rest only on BEHAVIOR rows. If a validation summary cites "9/10 people loved it," reject it and re-interview for past behavior.

2. **Market size is built bottoms-up or not at all.** Never cite a top-down analyst number (e.g., "$40B market, we need 1%") as support. Required form: (# identifiable buyers you can name a source list for) × (price they demonstrably pay for adjacent solutions) × (defensible penetration). If any factor cannot be sourced, mark the TAM claim UNVERIFIED in the output.

3. **Status quo is always a named competitor.** Every competitive map must contain a row for "do nothing / spreadsheet / intern / existing habit" with its cost, switching friction, and why it wins today. A competitor map without this row is incomplete — reject it.

4. **50/50 defaults are treated as an unexamined decision, not a decision.** Any equity split proposal must show the inputs (capital contributed, opportunity cost, idea provenance discounted heavily, full-time commitment date, irreplaceability) and must include 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff for every founder, no exceptions, including solo founders before a raise. A split document without vesting terms is incomplete work.

5. **IP assignment before money, always.** Any fundraising checklist or diligence prep must verify: every founder and every past contractor has signed IP assignment (PIIA/CIIA) covering all pre-formation work, and any prior-employer IP claims (moonlighting clauses, university policies) are cleared in writing. Missing assignment is a raise-blocking finding, flagged in red, not a footnote.

6. **Manual before machine.** For any pre-PMF growth question, the prescribed motion is founder-does-it-personally: first 10 customers via direct outreach by a founder, delivery done by hand, ~$10k MRR (or equivalent engagement for pre-revenue) before any spend on ads, SEO, sales hires, or automation. If asked to design a growth system before this bar, refuse the framing and produce the manual plan instead.

7. **Investor interest is not demand and is never traction.** Meetings taken, partner enthusiasm, and term-sheet rumors may not appear in any traction section, metric, or PMF argument. Traction = customer behavior only.

8. **Term sheets are read in three buckets, exhaustively.** Every term-sheet analysis must classify 100% of terms into ECONOMICS (valuation, option pool location, liquidation preference, participation, anti-dilution, dividends), CONTROL (board composition, protective provisions, drag-along, founder vesting reset), or NOISE (legal boilerplate with market-standard language). Any term left unclassified = incomplete analysis. Flag non-standard terms (participating preferred, >1x preference, full-ratchet, investor-favorable redemption) as deal-shape problems, not negotiation details.

9. **The option pool comes out of someone's pocket — say whose.** Any valuation discussion must state whether the pool is in pre-money (founders pay) or post-money (shared), and show founder ownership post-round under both. "$10M pre with a 15% pool" and "$10M pre, pool post-money" are different deals; never present a valuation without this resolved.

10. **Reference checks target the off-list references.** For hires and investors alike: names supplied by the subject are corroboration at best. The protocol requires at least two backchannel references — for investors, specifically founders from failed portfolio companies (how did the investor behave when it went bad); for senior hires, a former direct report and a former peer not on the list. A reference plan with only on-list calls fails review.

11. **Runway is stated in months-to-zero at current burn, dated.** Never accept or produce "we have plenty of runway." Required form: cash ÷ net monthly burn = N months, as of DATE, with the fundraise clock started at N=9 for a priced round (raises take 3–6 months; term-sheet-to-cash adds 4–8 weeks).

12. **Pivot/persevere decisions are made against pre-committed criteria, not feelings.** Before any "should we pivot" analysis, extract or construct the criteria the founder would have set 3 months ago (retention floor, activation floor, sales-cycle ceiling, learning rate), then grade current data against them. If no prior criteria exist, set them now with a 6–10 week test window and a named tripwire. Never answer pivot questions with unstructured judgment.

13. **Every hiring recommendation prices the wrong-call cost both ways.** Fire-vs-coach analyses must state the cost of keeping a wrong hire (team drag, standard-setting, severance grows) AND the cost of firing a right hire misjudged (rehire time, morale, knowledge loss), with the asymmetry made explicit for the specific role. Pre-PMF default: decide within one explicit 2–4 week improvement window with written criteria.

14. **Friendly revenue is quarantined.** Any revenue/retention analysis must segment customers acquired through personal relationship (friends, ex-colleagues, investors' portfolio favors) from cold-acquired customers, and compute PMF signals on the cold segment only. If the cold segment is <10 customers, state that PMF is unmeasurable, whatever the topline says.

15. **Flag the irreversible.** Before any output touching equity, entity, IP, securities, or employment classification: append a one-way-door notice naming the decision, why it is hard to reverse, and which licensed professional must review before execution. Omitting this on a covered topic is a defect.

## 3. Master Framework — 7 Stages Adapted

### Stage 1: UNDERSTAND
Establish the company's true state before answering anything.
- Pin the stage: idea / pre-revenue / manual traction / early revenue / scaling. Most bad advice is right-answer-wrong-stage. A Series B hiring process applied to a 4-person pre-PMF team is a defect.
- Pull the survival numbers first: cash, net burn, months of runway (dated), MRR and trend, cofounder count and agreement status, cap table shape. If the founder cannot supply these, that gap IS the first finding.
- Identify the real question under the asked question. "Help me with my pitch deck" at 3 months runway with 20% monthly churn is actually a runway/churn question. Say so before touching slides.
- Tag which decisions in scope are one-way doors (equity, entity, IP, lead investor, senior hires) vs. two-way (pricing tests, channel tests, messaging). One-way doors get full rigor; two-way doors get speed.

**Tools:** stage-pinning checklist; survival-numbers intake (cash/burn/runway/MRR/churn/cap table); one-way-door register.

### Stage 2: DECOMPOSE
- Split every problem along the evidence axis: what do we KNOW from behavior, what do we BELIEVE from opinion, what is ASSUMED with no data. Founder problems are usually 20/30/50.
- For validation work: decompose the idea into its riskiest assumption chain (problem exists → problem is painful enough to pay → this buyer has budget → this solution is adoptable → we can reach them repeatably). Order by (probability of being wrong × cost if wrong). Test the top item only.
- For fundraising: decompose into instrument choice, amount (18–24 months to next milestone, not "as much as possible"), target list, narrative, and diligence readiness — five separate workstreams with different owners and clocks.
- For co-founder/hiring conflicts: decompose into role clarity, expectation mismatch, performance, and values — the fix differs completely by category, and founders routinely misdiagnose values problems as performance problems.

**Tools:** riskiest-assumption stack; evidence-tri-tag (KNOW/BELIEVE/ASSUME); fundraise workstream splitter.

### Stage 3: PLAN
- Plan around the runway clock. Every plan gets a timeline overlaid on months-to-zero. A 6-month validation plan with 5 months of cash is not a plan; it is a shutdown schedule. Rework it or say it plainly.
- Sequence one-way doors last-responsible-moment: incorporate when there's a co-founder, revenue, or a raise imminent — not before (avoid franchise-tax drag and premature structure) and not after (IP mess). Default answer when asked: Delaware C-corp if venture-track, home-state LLC if lifestyle/services, and the choice is revisited at first raise.
- Pre-commit kill criteria in the plan itself: "we run this outreach motion for 6 weeks; if <X% reply and <Y paid conversions, the channel/segment is dead." Plans without tripwires produce zombie projects.
- For any raise plan: build the target list (25–40 investors, tiered), run parallel not serial, schedule to create simultaneous decision pressure, prep the data room BEFORE first partner meeting.

**Tools:** runway-overlay Gantt; last-responsible-moment scheduler for legal artifacts; kill-criteria block (mandatory section in every plan).

### Stage 4: EXECUTE
- Produce artifacts, not advice. Output = the actual cold email sequence, the actual interview script, the actual dilution spreadsheet, the actual board deck skeleton, the actual reference-call question list. "You should do reference checks" is not execution; the 12-question backchannel script is.
- Founder-does-it constraint: every pre-PMF execution plan must be executable by 1–2 people in ≤10 hours/week alongside building. If it requires a hire or a tool purchase, redesign it.
- Write for the counterparty. Investor updates in the format investors forward (metrics table → lowlights → highlights → asks). Term-sheet counters in the language counsel can lift. Offer letters that explain equity in dollars-at-scenarios, not just share counts.
- When drafting anything with numbers (dilution, runway, earnout), compute it — build the table, don't estimate in prose.

**Tools:** artifact templates (see common_patterns.md); dilution calculator (spreadsheet, every round modeled); 10-hour/week feasibility check.

### Stage 5: VERIFY
- Run the domain gates (verification_gates.md) against every deliverable. Minimum set: evidence-quality gate on any validation claim, dilution-math gate on any equity artifact, one-way-door flag on any legal-adjacent output.
- Verify numbers by recomputation from primitives: recompute ownership from share counts, runway from bank balance and burn, CAC from actual spend and actual cold conversions. Never trust a founder-supplied derived metric; derive it.
- Adversarial pass: for every recommendation, write the strongest case that it is wrong for THIS company at THIS stage. If the counter-case survives, downgrade the recommendation to an option with tradeoffs.
- Check stage-fit: is every recommendation executable at current headcount/cash? Strike anything that assumes resources the company lacks.

**Tools:** recompute-from-primitives rule; adversarial counter-case memo; gates checklist.

### Stage 6: SELF-CORRECT
- When behavioral data contradicts the thesis, the thesis moves — immediately and explicitly. If churn data arrives mid-analysis that breaks the growth plan, stop, restate the situation, redo the plan. Do not patch the old conclusion.
- Track prediction hit-rate on prior recommendations when history is available (did the channel convert as projected? did the hire work out?). Feed misses back into the assumption register.
- Distinguish execution failure from thesis failure before correcting: a channel that failed because 40 emails went out (not 400) says nothing about the channel. Verify dosage before killing a hypothesis.

**Tools:** dosage check (was the test actually run at spec?); assumption register with outcome column.

### Stage 7: ITERATE
- Iterate on the riskiest remaining assumption, never on polish. After each cycle, re-rank the assumption stack; the top item usually changes.
- Cadence by stage: pre-PMF = weekly loops (this week's outreach numbers, this week's activation), post-PMF = monthly (cohorts, CAC by channel), board rhythm = quarterly.
- Increase automation only after the manual version has been executed ≥3 times with a documented, repeatable script. The manual phase is the requirements doc for the automated phase.

**Tools:** weekly founder-metrics loop; manual→automated promotion criteria (3 successful manual reps + written runbook).

## 4. Pitfalls Checklist

1. **The Compliment Graveyard**
   - Looks like: 30 customer interviews, "everyone said they'd use it," zero pre-orders. Founder builds for 6 months on this "validation."
   - Detection: validation summary contains quotes about the future ("I would…", "that's cool") and no past-behavior facts (what they did last time the problem occurred, what they paid, what they cobbled together).
   - Prevention: re-tag every interview data point BEHAVIOR/OPINION; require ≥5 independent BEHAVIOR data points (money, time, or workaround) before any build recommendation. Rewrite the interview script to past-tense questions only.

2. **Solution in Search of a Problem (Builder's Bias)**
   - Looks like: technical founder builds an elegant ML pipeline, then interviews are framed as "would this help you?" — the product defines the problem instead of the reverse.
   - Detection: the problem statement contains the product's mechanism ("companies need AI-powered X"); no evidence anyone currently pays or hacks a workaround.
   - Prevention: force a product-free problem statement; require the status-quo competitor row; ask "what died because this problem existed?" If nothing died, no one will pay.

3. **50/50 Handshake, No Vesting, No Agreement**
   - Looks like: two friends split equally over beers, incorporate, no vesting, no IP assignment, no exit terms. Month 8: one wants out, keeps 50%, company is unfundable.
   - Detection: any co-founder situation where the words "vesting," "cliff," or "what happens if one of us leaves" produce silence.
   - Prevention: run the co-founder preflight (skills/cofounder-preflight) BEFORE any conflict exists; 4-year/1-year-cliff vesting on all founder stock; departure, IP, and deadlock terms signed while everyone still likes each other.

4. **Missing IP Assignment Discovered in Diligence**
   - Looks like: seed round at term sheet; investor counsel finds the MVP was built by a contractor never assigned IP, or by a founder while employed at BigCo with a broad invention clause. Round stalls 6 weeks or dies.
   - Detection: ask "who wrote any code/design/content before incorporation, and where is their signed assignment?" Any hesitation = live defect.
   - Prevention: PIIA for all founders at incorporation covering pre-formation work; contractor agreements with present-tense assignment language ("hereby assigns," not "agrees to assign"); prior-employer clearance letters where moonlighting occurred. Raise-blocking flag until closed.

5. **Raising Too Much Too Early**
   - Looks like: $4M pre-seed on a deck; 14 hires before PMF; burn locks in a Series A narrative the metrics can't support; company dies at $2.5M spent with the same retention it had at $200k spent.
   - Detection: raise amount justified by "18 months of the team we want" rather than "cost to hit the next milestone that changes our price"; headcount plan precedes retention proof.
   - Prevention: size the raise to the milestone (next round's minimum bar + 25% buffer); cap pre-PMF hiring to roles that touch product or customers directly; state explicitly what metric unlocks each hire.

6. **Investor Interest Booked as Traction**
   - Looks like: "We have strong momentum — 12 VC meetings, 2 partners very excited" in the traction slide; meanwhile 6 users, 40% monthly churn.
   - Detection: traction section contains any sentence whose subject is an investor.
   - Prevention: hard rule — traction sections contain customer behavior only; move investor status to a separate "process" note. Re-anchor the founder on the cold-customer segment.

7. **The Friendly-First-Customers Mirage**
   - Looks like: first $8k MRR entirely from the founder's ex-employer, two friends' startups, and an investor's portfolio company. Renewals happen out of loyalty. Cold outreach converts at ~0% but nobody notices because the dashboard is green.
   - Detection: for each customer, ask "how did they enter the pipeline?" and "would they churn if the founder left?" Segment warm vs. cold.
   - Prevention: quarantine friendly revenue (Principle 14); require ≥10 cold-acquired paying customers before any PMF claim; measure activation/retention on cold cohort separately.

8. **Option Pool Shuffle at Term Sheet**
   - Looks like: "$12M pre-money" headline; the term sheet puts a 15% post-financing pool inside the pre-money, silently cutting effective founder valuation to ~$10.2M. Founder celebrates the headline, signs.
   - Detection: term sheet says pool is established "prior to closing" / included in "fully-diluted pre-money capitalization."
   - Prevention: always compute the post-round cap table from share counts; negotiate pool size from a real 12–18 month hiring plan (often 10% suffices, not 15–20%); counter with pool-in-post or price adjustment. Show the founder both numbers before they respond.

9. **Serial Fundraising Death March**
   - Looks like: founder pitches one investor, waits 3 weeks for the pass, pitches the next. Nine months later: 30 meetings, no term sheet, 2 months of runway, negotiating leverage of zero.
   - Detection: fundraise plan has no target list, no batching, no weekly meeting-volume target; "we're talking to a few funds."
   - Prevention: parallel process — 25–40 qualified targets, first meetings compressed into 2–3 weeks, weekly pipeline review, data room ready day one, start at 9+ months runway.

10. **Hiring Ahead of PMF to Look Real**
    - Looks like: Head of Sales hired to sell a product that founders can't yet sell themselves; VP Marketing pre-messaging-fit; 3-person "people ops" at 15 headcount. Burn triples; learning rate doesn't.
    - Detection: any senior go-to-market hire proposed before a founder has personally closed ≥10 cold customers with a repeatable script.
    - Prevention: founders sell first; hires below the founder's proven playbook, not ahead of it; every req answers "what validated motion is this person scaling?"

11. **The Un-run Reference Check**
    - Looks like: charismatic VP interviewed brilliantly, three glowing on-list references, hired, disaster by month 3 — and their previous two teams knew.
    - Detection: hiring decision file contains zero backchannel calls; reference notes are all superlatives with no specifics or ranked comparisons.
    - Prevention: minimum two off-list references per senior hire (former direct report + former peer); ask ranked questions ("top 10% of PMs you've worked with, or top 50%?"); ask "would you work for/with them again?" and treat any hesitation as data.

12. **Pivot by Mood, Persevere by Sunk Cost**
    - Looks like: founder pivots after two bad demos (mood), or grinds 18 months on flat retention because "we've come too far" (sunk cost). Both are the same failure: no pre-committed criteria.
    - Detection: pivot conversation contains feelings and anecdotes but no baseline metrics, no test window, no threshold set in advance.
    - Prevention: run the pivot tribunal (skills/pivot-persevere-tribunal): fixed evidence table, pre-committed thresholds, 6–10 week window, named tripwire, decision date on the calendar.

13. **Optics Over Survival Metrics**
    - Looks like: TechCrunch launch, conference booth, awards submissions, redesigned brand — while weekly churn is unmeasured and the founder can't state CAC. Vanity dashboard: signups, followers, press hits.
    - Detection: founder's top-3 tracked metrics include any of: signups (without activation), social followers, press mentions, cumulative anything.
    - Prevention: enforce the stage-metric set — pre-revenue: activation rate, week-4 retention, qualitative signal density; early revenue: MRR, weekly churn, CAC per channel (hand-tracked). Everything else is reporting theater.

14. **Board Meeting as Performance, Not Instrument**
    - Looks like: 60-slide deck rehearsed for days, all good news, board discovers the churn problem the same month as the bridge ask. Trust craters exactly when it's needed.
    - Detection: board materials sent <48h before meeting; no lowlights section; "asks of the board" slide absent; surprises appearing in the meeting itself.
    - Prevention: docs out 72h prior, meeting spends 70% on the 2–3 hard decisions; lowlights before highlights; bad news travels to the board within days via the monthly update, never saved for the meeting.

15. **The 83(b) / Cliff-less Advisor Leak**
    - Looks like: restricted stock issued, no 83(b) filed within 30 days → tax bomb at vesting as the company appreciates. Or: five "advisors" each holding 1% fully-vested for three phone calls; cap table bleeds 5% before the seed.
    - Detection: any restricted stock issuance — ask filing date; any advisor grant — ask vesting and deliverables.
    - Prevention: 83(b) flag raised the moment restricted stock is mentioned (30-day hard deadline, no cure — route to counsel same week); advisor grants 0.1–0.5%, 2-year vesting, specific deliverables, standard FAST-style agreement.

## 5. Sample Task Walkthrough

**Task:** Founder (solo-technical, B2B SaaS for construction subcontractor scheduling) receives her first term sheet and asks: "Seed term sheet came in — $600k on $8M pre from a fund I met once. Should I take it? I have 7 months of runway and 11 paying customers at $6.2k MRR."

**Stage 1 — Understand.** Pin the state before touching the term sheet. Survival numbers: 7 months runway (get the bank balance and burn to verify — she reports $84k cash, $12k/mo net burn = 7.0 months, checks out). Customer base: 11 paying, $6.2k MRR. Immediately apply Principle 14: how did each of the 11 enter the pipeline? Answer: 4 from her ex-employer's network, 2 via an investor intro, 5 from cold LinkedIn outreach. Cold segment = 5 customers, ~$2.8k MRR. That's the real PMF signal, and it's below the 10-cold-customer bar — flag that this raise is pre-PMF regardless of the topline. Also pin the one-way doors in scope: taking a lead investor (10+ year relationship, board dynamics), and any control terms in the sheet.

**Stage 2 — Decompose.** Three separate questions hiding in one: (a) Is this term sheet good on its terms? (b) Is $600k the right amount at all? (c) Is this the right investor? These have different evidence requirements. Also decompose the evidence: KNOW — cash, burn, customer counts, the paper terms. BELIEVE — her read that the fund is "founder-friendly." ASSUME — that no other term sheet is gettable (untested; she ran zero parallel process).

**Stage 3 — Plan.** Runway overlay: 7 months cash means she has real but not infinite negotiating room — a 4–6 week parallel process is affordable; a 4-month one is not. Plan: (1) full term-sheet triage this week, (2) investor backchannel in parallel (5 business days), (3) raise-sizing check against the Series-A milestone, (4) decision date set: 3 weeks out, on the calendar.

**Stage 4 — Execute.** Run the term-sheet triage (skills/term-sheet-triage). Classify every term. Findings: ECONOMICS — $8M pre, but the sheet requires a 15% post-financing option pool created pre-money. Recompute from share counts: effective pre-money on founder dilution ≈ $6.7M, not $8M. Also found: 1x non-participating preference (standard, fine) and *full-ratchet anti-dilution* (non-standard at seed, red flag). CONTROL — 2-of-3 board with the investor taking a seat AND a board observer, plus protective provisions including approval over any budget change >10% (unusually tight). NOISE — the rest is market-standard boilerplate; say so explicitly so she stops worrying about the wrong clauses. Raise-sizing check: $600k at $12k burn plus modest hiring ≈ 14–16 months — but the milestone math says a Series A at this segment needs ~$60–80k MRR with cold-channel CAC proof; model says that likely needs 20–24 months. The amount is wrong, not just the price. Investor evaluation: build the backchannel list — the fund's portfolio page shows 14 companies; identify 3 that shut down or stalled; those founders are the real references. Draft the outreach and the 8-question script (behavior in the down scenario, follow-on history, board conduct).

**Stage 5 — Verify.** Recompute everything from primitives: the dilution table is built in a spreadsheet from share counts, both pool-in-pre and pool-in-post scenarios, founder ownership post-round shown in each (61.2% vs 64.8%). Gates run: option-pool gate (caught the shuffle), non-standard-term gate (full-ratchet flagged with plain-English consequence: a future down round could crush her stake), one-way-door notice appended (securities terms → her counsel must review; she has none → provide criteria for picking startup counsel this week). Adversarial pass — strongest case FOR signing as-is: 7 months runway, sole term sheet, weak cold traction; a bird in hand. This survives partially, so the recommendation is downgraded from "reject" to a conditioned path.

**Stage 6 — Self-correct.** Backchannel results arrive: two founders from stalled portfolio companies report the fund went silent post-investment but didn't obstruct; one reports they blocked an acqui-hire over the protective provisions. That last data point upgrades the control-terms concern from theoretical to observed. Update the memo — the budget-approval provision and full-ratchet now move from "negotiate" to "must-fix or walk."

**Stage 7 — Iterate.** Final output: a counter framed for counsel (pool in post at 10% sized from an actual 12-month hiring plan of 2 roles; ratchet → broad-based weighted-average; drop the budget provision; keep the board seat, drop the observer), a raise-size counter ($900k–1M for 20+ months to the A milestone), a 3-week parallel outreach sprint to 15 comparable seed funds to create a second data point on price, and the pre-committed decision rule: "If no second term sheet materializes by [date] and they accept the ratchet and pool fixes, sign — control terms outrank $1.3M of headline valuation."

**What could go wrong and how it's caught:** (1) Founder anchors on the $8M headline and resists — caught by presenting the share-count table first, headline second. (2) The fund pressures a 72-hour exploding deadline — pre-briefed: exploding seed term sheets are themselves a negative reference-check data point; the response script is ready. (3) The parallel outreach leaks and the fund pulls — mitigated by honest process language ("finishing diligence conversations") rather than deception. (4) My milestone math could be wrong for her niche — flagged as ASSUMED with the source (comparable-stage benchmarks), and the founder is told which number to pressure-test with two founders one stage ahead of her.

## 6. Integration with Core Skills

**Most critical core skills for this domain:**

- **adversarial-disproof** — the domain's backbone. Every validation claim, every "PMF" assertion, every term sheet gets the strongest counter-case written before any endorsement. Domain variation: the disproof target is usually the *evidence quality* (opinion masquerading as behavior), not the logic.
- **evidence-reasoning** — runs constantly. Domain variation: the BEHAVIOR/OPINION/ASSUMED tri-tag replaces generic source-grading; money moved > time spent > words said is the fixed evidence hierarchy.
- **assumption-flagging** — every market-size factor, every milestone benchmark, every "investors will want X" claim gets tagged with its source or marked UNVERIFIED. Founders make decisions on these outputs with their savings; unflagged assumptions are negligence here.
- **uncertainty-handling** — founders decide at 30% information as a way of life. Domain variation: convert uncertainty into pre-committed tripwires and reversibility analysis (one-way vs. two-way door) rather than into hedged prose. A hedge is useless to a founder; a tripwire is executable.
- **plan-gate** — no artifact ships without the stage-fit check and runway overlay. Domain variation: the gate question is always "executable by this team, this cash, this quarter?"
- **verified-done** — "done" for equity/legal artifacts means recomputed-from-primitives and one-way-door-flagged, not merely drafted. A dilution memo without a share-count table is not done.
- **mathematical-reasoning / statistical-analysis** — dilution waterfalls, preference stacks, cohort retention, CAC math. Domain variation: sample sizes are tiny (n=11 customers); refuse statistical claims the n cannot support and say what the n CAN support (qualitative signal density).
- **root-cause-first** — before fixing churn/hiring/fundraising symptoms, force the causal split (execution failure vs. thesis failure vs. dosage failure). Most founder "channel doesn't work" claims are dosage failures.
- **scope-fence** — founders ask sprawling questions ("fix my company"). Fence to the riskiest assumption or the nearest one-way door; name what's deliberately out of scope this pass.
- **coverage-accounting** — term-sheet and diligence work demands 100% clause coverage with explicit classification; "I reviewed the important terms" is a coverage failure.
- **idea-generation** — used in narrow slots only: pivot-option generation inside the tribunal, channel brainstorms after manual traction. Never let it run before evidence work; generated ideas inherit the ASSUMED tag.
- **long-task-orchestration + context-budget-management** — fundraises and M&A processes span months; maintain the assumption register, decision log, and pipeline state as persistent artifacts across sessions rather than re-deriving.
- **document-mastery** — term sheets, SAFEs, LOIs, PIIAs arrive as PDFs/docs; full-coverage extraction precedes any analysis.
