# Marketing & Sales — Common Task Patterns

## 1. Diagnosing a Paid-Ad Performance Drop (Creative Fatigue vs. Auction Shift)
**Trigger:** CPA rises >20% or CTR falls >15% week-over-week on a previously stable campaign, with no landing-page or offer change.
**Approach:** 1. Segment the drop: by campaign → ad set → creative → placement. Isolate whether it is universal (auction/seasonality) or concentrated (creative/audience). 2. Check frequency: if frequency >3-4 on Meta or impression share stable on Google while CTR falls, it is creative fatigue. 3. Check CPM trend: rising CPM with flat CTR = auction pressure, not your fault — verify against competitor activity and seasonal benchmarks. 4. Check conversion rate downstream: if CTR is flat but CVR fell, the problem is post-click (page, offer, tracking), not the ad. 5. Ship the fix: rotate 3+ net-new concepts (not resizes) for fatigue; adjust bids/audiences for auction shifts; audit pixel/tag firing for tracking breaks.
**Checklist:** [ ] Drop segmented to level (account/campaign/creative) [ ] Frequency and CPM trends pulled [ ] CTR vs CVR decomposed [ ] Tracking verified firing (test conversion) [ ] Fix matched to root cause, not "refresh everything"
**Variation:** Attribution-window changes or iOS/privacy updates masquerade as performance drops. Before touching creative, confirm the measurement layer didn't change (window settings, consent-mode rollout, pixel version).

## 2. Building a Messaging Hierarchy for a Launch or Repositioning
**Trigger:** New product/feature launch, new segment entry, or sales reporting "prospects don't get what we do."
**Approach:** 1. Pull 10+ recent won/lost call recordings or notes; extract the words customers use for the problem (JTBD language, not your feature names). 2. Define the positioning statement: for [segment] who [struggle with X], [product] is the [category] that [key differentiator], unlike [alternative]. 3. Cascade: one core message → 3 pillars (each = claim + proof point + customer quote) → channel-specific expressions (homepage H1, ad hook, SDR opener, deck slide 2). 4. Pressure-test each pillar: can a competitor say the exact same sentence? If yes, sharpen or cut. 5. Ship a one-page messaging doc; require sales and marketing sign-off on the SAME doc before any asset is written.
**Template:** Segment: ___ | Problem (their words): ___ | Category: ___ | Differentiator: ___ | Alternative displaced: ___ | Pillar 1/2/3: claim + proof + quote | Banned words: ___
**Variation:** Hierarchy built from internal opinion instead of customer language — reads as "innovative, seamless, all-in-one." If the proof column is empty or the claims survive the competitor-swap test, restart from call recordings.

## 3. Cold Outbound Sequence Build (SDR/BDR)
**Trigger:** New territory, new ICP, or reply rate <2% on the existing sequence.
**Approach:** 1. Define the ICP trigger event (funding, hiring for role X, tech install, regulatory deadline) — sequence relevance dies without one. 2. Write email 1 with PAS: name the specific problem the trigger implies, agitate with a cost number or peer comparison, bridge to one-sentence value. Under 90 words, one CTA (interest-based, not "book 30 minutes"). 3. Build a 5-7 touch cadence across 12-18 days: email → call → LinkedIn → email (new angle, not "bumping this") → call → breakup email. Each email must carry a NEW reason to reply. 4. Verify deliverability prerequisites before send: SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass, domain warmed, <50/day/mailbox, list verified (bounce risk <2%). 5. Launch to 100-200 contacts; judge on reply rate and meeting rate, not opens (open tracking is unreliable post-MPP).
**Checklist:** [ ] Trigger event defined [ ] Email 1 <90 words, single CTA [ ] Each touch adds new information [ ] SPF/DKIM/DMARC verified [ ] List bounce-checked [ ] Reply/meeting rate targets set (>3% reply, >1% meeting)
**Variation:** Personalization theater — "Loved your post about X" followed by a generic pitch. Personalize the PROBLEM inference from the trigger, not the flattery line. Also watch volume creep: scaling sends before reply rate proves the message burns the domain.

## 4. Discovery Call Preparation and Qualification (MEDDIC Pass)
**Trigger:** Inbound demo request or SDR-booked meeting hits an AE calendar; also re-run when a deal stalls >2 weeks in stage.
**Approach:** 1. Pre-call: pull firmographics, tech stack, recent trigger events, and the prospect's role vs. the buying committee map. Write 3 hypotheses about their pain. 2. On call, run SPIN order: Situation (max 3 questions — research should have answered the rest), Problem, Implication ("what does that cost you per quarter?"), Need-payoff. 3. Score MEDDIC live: Metrics (quantified impact), Economic buyer (named), Decision criteria, Decision process (steps + dates), Identify pain, Champion (has power AND is selling internally). 4. Close the call with a mutual action plan: next step, date, and who else joins. No next step booked on the call = deal at risk. 5. Log MEDDIC fields in CRM same day; any field blank after call 2 = flag in forecast.
**Template (CRM notes):** Metric: ___ | EB: ___ (met? Y/N) | Criteria: ___ | Process/dates: ___ | Pain (their words): ___ | Champion: ___ + evidence they're selling internally: ___ | Next step + date: ___
**Variation:** Happy-ears qualification — champion enthusiasm mistaken for a deal. A champion who cannot name the economic buyer or the approval process is a coach, not a champion. Downgrade the forecast category until EB access is confirmed.

## 5. A/B Test Design for Conversion Assets (Landing Page, Email, CTA)
**Trigger:** Conversion rate below benchmark on a high-traffic asset, or a stakeholder proposes a redesign "because it feels stale."
**Approach:** 1. Form a causal hypothesis from evidence (heatmaps, session recordings, exit surveys, funnel step data): "Visitors bounce at the form because 11 fields signal effort" — not "let's try a new hero." 2. Change ONE variable class per test (headline, offer framing, CTA copy, form length). 3. Compute required sample size BEFORE launch: baseline CVR, minimum detectable effect (target relative lift ≥15% for small samples), 95% confidence, 80% power. If the asset can't reach sample in 4 weeks, test a bigger swing or a higher-traffic page. 4. Run full weeks only (weekday/weekend mix); no peeking-based stops — pre-commit the end date or use sequential testing properly. 5. Record result in a test log win or lose: hypothesis, variant, sample, lift, decision. Losses are inventory, not failures.
**Checklist:** [ ] Hypothesis cites evidence source [ ] One variable class changed [ ] Sample size calculated pre-launch [ ] End date pre-committed [ ] Guardrail metric set (e.g., lead quality, not just lead volume) [ ] Result logged regardless of outcome
**Variation:** Winner's-curse shipping — declaring victory at p<0.05 on day 3 with 40 conversions/arm. Also: optimizing the primary metric while degrading the guardrail (form-field removal lifts submits but floods sales with junk MQLs). Always pair the conversion metric with a downstream quality metric.

## 6. Funnel and Attribution Audit (Marketing-Sourced Pipeline Review)
**Trigger:** Quarterly review, board prep, or the recurring fight: "marketing says MQLs are up, sales says pipeline is down."
**Approach:** 1. Reconcile definitions first: pull the written MQL/SQL/SQO definitions and verify both teams' dashboards use the same fields and date logic. Most "attribution problems" are definition drift. 2. Rebuild the funnel with volumes AND conversion rates per stage: visitor → lead → MQL → SQL → opp → closed-won, split by channel. 3. Compare attribution models side by side: run last-touch AND first-touch AND data-driven/linear on the same closed-won set. Channels that only look good in one model are suspect (branded search under last-touch is the classic inflator). 4. Compute unit economics per channel: CAC (fully loaded, including salaries), LTV:CAC, CAC payback months. Kill or fix channels with payback > policy threshold. 5. Deliver findings as: 3 channels to scale, 2 to fix, 1 to kill — with the stage-level bottleneck named for each "fix."
**Template:** Channel | Spend | Leads | MQL | SQL | Opps | Won | CAC | Payback | Verdict (scale/fix/kill) | Bottleneck stage
**Variation:** Vanity-metric substitution — reporting MQL growth while SQL→opp conversion collapsed, meaning marketing shifted to lower-intent volume. Any stage conversion that dropped >20% while upstream volume grew is the real story; lead the readout with it.
